Arriving in Nairobi

Most international flights land at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA), south-east of the city centre. Domestic and safari flights often use Wilson Airport on Langata Road. The Nairobi Expressway connects the JKIA area with the CBD and Westlands and can save a lot of time outside peak hours (tolls apply).

Entry requirements

Most visitors to Kenya need to apply online for an Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA) before travelling. East African Community citizens and some other nationalities are exempt. Rules, fees and exemptions change, so always check the official government portal before booking flights, and avoid unofficial agents that add fees.

Your passport should be valid for at least six months. Depending on where you travel from, you may need a yellow fever vaccination certificate — check with your doctor and airline.

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice.

Getting around Nairobi

  • Ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Bolt are widely used and are the easiest option for visitors.
  • Taxis are available at hotels and the airport — agree the fare before you set off.
  • Matatus (shared minibuses) are cheap and colourful but can be confusing for first-timers.
  • The SGR train runs from the Nairobi Terminus near Syokimau towards Mombasa — a comfortable way to reach the coast.
  • Traffic can be heavy during rush hours — plan extra time, especially for flights.

Money & payments

Kenya's currency is the Kenyan shilling (KES). Cards are accepted in most hotels, malls and larger restaurants, and ATMs are common. M-Pesa mobile money is used almost everywhere, from taxis to market stalls. Carry some cash for smaller purchases and tips.

SIM cards & internet

Buying a local SIM (for example from Safaricom or Airtel) gives you cheap data and access to M-Pesa. You'll need your passport to register it. Many visitors use eSIMs too. Most accommodation we list includes Wi-Fi.

Weather & what to pack

Nairobi sits at roughly 1,700–1,800 m above sea level, so the climate is mild all year — warm days and cool evenings. The main rainy seasons are usually March–May (long rains) and October–December (short rains). Pack layers, a light jacket, comfortable shoes and sunscreen.

Staying safe

  • Use ride-hailing apps or trusted taxis, especially at night.
  • Keep phones and valuables out of sight in busy areas.
  • Choose accommodation with good security — look for “24/7 security” in listings.
  • Only pay for bookings using instructions from our official WhatsApp number.

Things to do in and around Nairobi

Nairobi National Park

Wildlife safaris with the city skyline in the background — a world first.

Giraffe Centre & Sheldrick Wildlife Trust

Meet Rothschild's giraffes in Karen and visit the famous elephant orphanage.

Karura Forest

Walking and cycling trails, waterfalls and caves — a green escape in the city.

Museums & heritage

Nairobi National Museum, Karen Blixen Museum and the Nairobi Railway Museum.

Further afield, popular trips include Lake Naivasha, Hell's Gate National Park and the Maasai Mara.

Useful Swahili phrases

English is widely spoken, but a few words of Swahili go a long way:

  • Jambo / Habari — Hello / How are you?
  • Karibu — Welcome
  • Asante (sana) — Thank you (very much)
  • Tafadhali — Please
  • Pole pole — Slowly, take it easy

Practical information

  • Time zone: East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+3, no daylight saving.
  • Electricity: 240 V, UK-style Type G plugs.
  • Emergency numbers: 999 or 112.
  • Language: English and Swahili are official languages.
